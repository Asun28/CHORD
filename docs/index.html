Community Hub Operations and Response Desk · AI Hackathon 2026, Wellington

The hub playbook already exists. We made it read the news.

Every Community Emergency Hub in the Wellington region has an official response plan naming its water tank, its neighbouring hubs, its aged care facilities. They are PDFs, and nobody opens a PDF in an earthquake. CHORD compiles all 114 of them into a live operations desk that watches the national feeds, works out what is happening to your hub, and hands whoever walks through the door their role and their next three tasks.

What you get

Not a dashboard. A colleague for the person in the lanyard.

Aggregating feeds alone is a weather app. The product is organisational memory, and WREMO already wrote it 114 times. Delete the plan and the product stops working. That is how you know that is the product.

114 response plans, compiled into an operations board

Each hub's Community Response Plan names its emergency water tank, its four neighbouring hubs, its kit contents, its local places and its vulnerable population sites. CHORD extracts the facts into a structured store and keeps the prose for retrieval, so the same magnitude 5.8 produces different actions at different hubs. Because it read their plan.

Walk in, get a role, get three tasks

Two short questions at the door. Unfilled priority roles fill first, then tasks come from the fixed catalogue, never invented. Dave has a lanyard and a job before he reaches the front of the queue.

Ask Kea, the hub's playbook agent

Plain questions answered from the regional guide plus this hub's plan. Where is the water tank. What does the Hub Supervisor do. Structured lookups run in code first, so the answer is grounded even with no model attached, and the answer says which mode it used.

Age on the face of every fact

The system CHORD replaces was four weeks stale and never said a word. Here the age sits on the tile, not in a tooltip, and a tile visibly degrades when it passes its cadence. Kill a feed on stage and the board tells you.

Posture is rules, not vibes

Green, watch, act, respond is computed from trust tier, distance and severity. The model can reorder tasks and write prose. It cannot raise a severity level or invent an instruction.

Privacy gate before anything renders

Response plans name aged care facilities, early childhood centres and social housing. 940 of 4,130 assets are withheld from the public view, and plan prose is redacted on retrieval, not just in the asset table.

Local first, so it works when it matters

A native macOS app on a machine in the hall, pointed at LM Studio or Ollama over any OpenAI compatible endpoint. It degrades from live feeds to a cached brief to the VHF already in the kit.

One Mac in the hall. Every phone in the room.

The node runs a small HTTP server, so the machine holding the corpus serves a live board to any browser on the hall's own network. My shift, Situation, Needs and offers, Roster, Monitor, Public info and Ask Kea, on the device already in someone's pocket. No internet, no accounts, nothing to install. That is what stops the coordinator being a help desk: everybody can read the board at once.

How it works

One loop, not an event bus

125 hubs and a handful of events a day is not a throughput problem. Five steps feed each other on a cursor you can scrub, which is also how the replay demo works.

poll → CAP → hubone node per hall
IngestOne poll loop, per feed adapters
NormaliseOne CAP schema, trust tier, dedupe
MatchGeometry and plan, to this hub
ReasonPosture rules, then ranked tasks
DeliverBoard and Ask

Normalising to CAP, the standard MetService and NEMA already emit, buys three things at once: adding a feed becomes an adapter, adding a region becomes a config change, and retraction comes for free through msgType: Cancel and Update. Once you have pushed an alert to forty volunteers you have to be able to un-push it.

Why not a dashboard

A broadcast tells 40,000 people the same thing.

The incumbent products in this space are resident facing broadcast dashboards, deployed at 80 or more organisations, with no reasoning layer. They are good at telling everybody one thing. That is a different job.

Broadcast dashboard
  • One message to every resident, no reasoning
  • Shows data. Leaves the interpretation to you
  • Cloud default. Fails in the conditions it exists for
  • A status banner that can go four weeks stale silently
  • Knows nothing about your hall or who is in it
CHORD
  • The person in the Reception lanyard gets their next three tasks
  • Feed state read against this hub's own response plan
  • Local first. Degrades to cached, then radio
  • Every tile carries its source and its age, and says when it stops
  • Knows the water tank, the neighbours and the eight roles

We are not replacing the whiteboard. Paper needs no power and coordinators are right to trust it. We are replacing the queue of people standing behind the one person who has read the guide.

Anchored to Sendai, then to the goals

Sendai Framework Target G commits countries to increase access to multi hazard early warning and disaster risk information. That is this product, stated as an international commitment. The goals below follow from it, with an official indicator each rather than seventeen logos.

What is real today, and what is not

  • Built in two days at a hackathon. This is a working prototype, and it is advisory. The human decides.
  • The board, walk in flow, Ask Kea, posture rules, privacy gate and regional map run on a native macOS app against the ingested corpus.
  • The web board serves the hall over plain local HTTP. It binds to loopback by default. Serving it to the rest of the room is an opt in toggle and is not yet TLS, so it belongs on a hall's own network, never a public one.
  • The event timeline is fixture backed replay, not a live national picture. Both national CAP feeds were empty during the build, so a live only demo risks blank panels.
  • Live GeoNet, Metlink, GWRC and MetService adapters are a drop in behind a stable FeedAdapter protocol, not yet shipped.
  • Not yet validated: replay against historical events, scale across all 125 hubs, a deployment path with rollback, and a runbook for the day it is wrong.

What we need is not funding.

Publish the state of emergency declaration as data. Publish the national CAP feed URL that already exists and is already described as public. Put an open licence on the hub guides so every region can build on what Wellington already wrote. The engine is open. The plans stay with the agency accountable for them.
